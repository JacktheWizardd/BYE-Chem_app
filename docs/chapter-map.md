# BYE Chem chapter map

Our own beginner order (decided by the user, 2026-11-07). Every section names the matching sections of the class textbook, **Tro, *Chemistry: A Molecular Approach*, 6e** (list in `docs/research/M-tro-6e-contents.md`), so students can find the topic in their book.

## How to read it
- **Levels:**
  - **Intro** is a first chemistry course (PCC CHEM 022, C-ID CHEM 101).
  - **1A** is PCC CHEM 001A (C-ID CHEM 110). **1B** is CHEM 001B.
  - **AP U*n*** is a unit of the AP Chemistry course (2024 course description). The units are: 1 Atomic Structure and Properties, 2 Compound Structure and Properties, 3 Properties of Substances and Mixtures, 4 Chemical Reactions, 5 Kinetics, 6 Thermochemistry, 7 Equilibrium, 8 Acids and Bases, 9 Thermodynamics and Electrochemistry.
  - The book has no AP mapping, so the AP tags are ours, at unit level. Check them against the course description's topic list before writing AP-specific content.
- **Ready? You need:** the sections listed before a section is opened. They always come earlier in this order, and the app's "Ready?" check links back to them.
- **Tro x.1:** each chapter's opening story is folded into that chapter's first section.
- Titles and questions are ours. Never copy the book's wording.

## Where this order differs from Tro, and why
1. **Chapter 0, Maths for Chemistry, comes first.** Maths skill predicts success in general chemistry (README §3, principle 10). Logarithms, quadratics and graphs wait in Chapter 0 until a later "Ready?" check sends students there.
2. **The mole gets its own chapter (4).** Tro spreads it over §2.9 and §§3.8–3.10. Beginners need the mole as one counting idea, given extra time (principle 8), and the liked chapter page is built around the mole map.
3. **Bonds and shapes come before quantum (Chapter 9, then 10–12).** Valence electrons can be read from the group number, so beginners can draw Lewis structures and shapes without orbitals. The intro course (C-ID 101) has bonding and structure but no quantum. Chapters 10–12 then explain *why*, with orbitals, trends, lattice energy and hybridization.
4. **pH gets a first look in Reactions in Water (6.7).** The intro course needs acids, bases and pH long before Tro's Chapter 17.
5. **Solutions (14) comes before Solids and Materials (15).** Solutions builds straight on intermolecular forces, and Solids is the 1A chapter needed least.
6. **Organic (16) is kept short, as 1A's "introduction to organic"**, and closes the 1A half. Biochemistry (Tro 23) is not covered yet.
7. **Nonmetals and metals (Tro 24–25) are one chapter, "Elements Up Close" (24).** It comes just before transition metals (25), which leans on Tro 25.5's 3d metals.
