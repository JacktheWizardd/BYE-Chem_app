## Product notes with sources

### Khan Academy Chemistry / AP Chemistry
- Teaches from basics through AP topics: atomic structure, bonding, reactions, kinetics, thermodynamics, equilibrium. Official support posts say an AP Chemistry beta course was to replace the older course, built with mastery-based practice, quizzes, unit tests and a course challenge. Source: https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/bg/community/posts/360059875512/comments/360009867892 (dated; whether the full course is complete is UNVERIFIED).
- The main khanacademy.org chemistry page would not fetch (https://www.khanacademy.org/science/chemistry), so unit lists, "free" status and live ratings are UNVERIFIED by me. Khan Academy is widely known as free, but I did not confirm on a page.
- Gap seen: videos are explain-then-practice; no named-step reveal on worked examples confirmed.

### ALEKS (McGraw Hill)
- McGraw Hill describes adaptive modules, regenerating problem instances, explanations per problem, and drawing tools that flag errors such as octet violations. Source: https://www.mheducation.com/highered/highered/aleks-chemistry.html. Pricing: page directs to a sales rep; no current price found. Reseller listings show 18-week and 40-week codes with old list prices for math (for example $266.75 for an 18-week algebra card), not chemistry. Source: https://www.slugbooks.com/9781259607035-college-algebra-with-aleks-360-18-weeks.html. Treat as UNVERIFIED for chemistry.
- Complaints: Trustpilot shows 1.3/5 from 65 reviews, all 1 or 2 star (seen 2026-11-06), citing punitive progress resets, short/dense/confusing explanations, strict answer formatting, and time demands. Profile is unclaimed so sample is biased. Source: https://www.trustpilot.com/review/aleks.com
- Older campus voices: a Colorado State article reports many student complaints even as instructors saw better test performance (https://campustechnology.com/articles/2009/06/24/online-system-brings-individualized-learning-to-colorado-state-chem-lectures.aspx); a UT student op-ed called the placement assessment a heavy time cost for high school review (https://thedailytexan.com/2020/09/22/ut-chemistry-department-needs-to-address-its-disregard-of-aleks-test-scores).
- Use at community colleges: a Skagit Valley case study exists on McGraw Hill's site (https://www.mheducation.com/unitas/highered/discipline/chemistry/aleks-chemistry-case-study-skagit-valley.pdf), vendor-authored.

### PhET (CU Boulder)
- Free HTML5 simulations including Build an Atom and Molecule Polarity (search result summary; see https://phet.colorado.edu/en/search?q=Atoms). Strongest particle-level visuals among free tools.
- Research: students explored Molecule Polarity with no instructions and used most features, but implicit scaffolding may not be enough to understand what is happening; light guidance focuses students on content while heavy guidance shifts focus to the interface. Sources: https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2013/rp/c3rp20157k and https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2014/rp/c4rp00009a . This supports a "simulation plus short guided reasoning" gap.

## The gap

1. **Reveal-one-named-step worked examples are not what anyone sells.** Solvers give all steps at once and auto-generate them (Chem AI listing); videos narrate continuously (Organic Chemistry Tutor, Crash Course); ALEKS explanations are criticised as dense (Trustpilot). No source found does paced, named, tap-to-reveal steps.
2. **Particle pictures live in sims, disconnected from the calculation.** PhET is the best free visual tool, but research says implicit scaffolding often is not enough and students need guidance (RSC 2013/2014). Nothing found links a particle picture to each step of a stoichiometry or equilibrium problem.
3. **Beginners get either homework systems or videos, not a short reading path.** ALEKS and Mastering assume a course and assigned work; Khan and YouTube are long-form. A short Learn page with pictures aimed at adults returning to study was not found.
4. **Assigned platforms are punitive or expensive for the person using them.** ALEKS reviews describe progress resets and format strictness; Mastering is a separate code purchase (Pearson FAQ, retailer $146.65). A calm, low-stakes practice option is open ground, but note that these are institution-sold, so BYE Chem cannot replace them for credit.
5. **Solver apps encourage answer-copying and bill aggressively.** Weekly billing, clone apps, and reviews that praise "passing" rather than understanding (Chem AI listing). Photomath's listing names no chemistry.
6. **AI tutors are free and strong but unreliable on calculations and application.** Studies find computation and application errors in equilibrium and redox. A product with checked, authored worked examples can differentiate on trust.
7. **Chem 1A alignment is weak outside vendor platforms.** Khan AP Chem beta and Crash Course follow AP-style sequences; none found map to a community-college Chem 1A outline.
8. **Chemistry is thin in the polished consumer-learning apps.** Brilliant reviewers want more chemistry; MEL is kit-based and for ages 10-16+; Toca Lab is a child game.

## Risks (what could make BYE Chem redundant)

- **Free general AI** (ChatGPT and similar) already gives step-by-step help and is improving; if students trust it enough, an authored app must win on correctness, pacing and pictures.
- **Khan Academy** could finish its AP Chemistry course with better exercises and hints; free and well known. Live status UNVERIFIED.
- **ALEKS / Mastering lock-in:** if the student's class mandates them, BYE Chem is a supplement, not a replacement. Vendors could improve explanations (McGraw Hill already promotes per-problem explanations).
- **PhET plus a good worksheet** is free for instructors; BYE Chem's particle pictures must add stepwise reasoning, not just visuals.
- **Many small gaps in evidence:** I could not read Reddit, Khan's live pages, Mastering/OWL feature pages, or most store listings. A second pass with direct store pages and student forums should confirm the weaknesses above before building around them.
- **Solver app clones** show low barriers to entry and aggressive pricing; a niche beginner product may be hard to discover.
