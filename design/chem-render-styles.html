BYE Chem: three render styles, as real 3D

Each column runs the same two three.js scenes (air in a box, and a methane molecule) with a different material. These are the actual materials the engine would use: three.js 0.187, the same library and version BYE Math Engine already draws 3D with on phones (through expo-gl). Drag any view to turn it, pinch or scroll to zoom, and flip the air box between 2D and 3D.

Moods came from Pinterest searches for Japanese science prints, risograph posters, Japanese editorial grids and matte 3D renders; nothing is copied from a pin. Molecule atoms carry their letters, so colour is never the only clue.

  • Feels: calm, bookish, grown-up; closest to BYE’s Mincho headings.
  • Good for: Learn pages and chapter pictures.
  • Risk: dots can look busy with hundreds of particles; keep counts low or dots light.
In the engine: one ShaderMaterial: Lambert light, then screen-space stipple (darker side, more dots), plus an inverted-hull ink outline. Works on InstancedMesh, so hundreds of atoms are two draw calls. The same shader draws the 2D view with an overhead camera.

In the engine: a two-band toon ShaderMaterial with a screen-space halftone in the second ink, offset a couple of pixels for the off-register look. The cheapest of the three on a phone GPU.

  • Feels: precise and quiet; the closest match to BYE Math’s graphs.
  • Good for: measuring things (pressure, angles, distances).
  • Risk: the least distinctive alone; it leans on motion and light.
In the engine: stock MeshStandardMaterial (rough, not shiny) with hemisphere and key light, a hairline GridHelper floor. Nothing custom to maintain; it’s how BYE Math Engine already draws 3D.

They can mix, because a style is only a material and a background: Sumi ink for Learn pages and chapter pictures, Two-colour print for the Lab, and the Drafting table grid whenever something is measured. In the engine that’s one theme setting; the simulation underneath doesn’t change. 2D is the same scene seen straight on with depth locked, so every style works in both.
