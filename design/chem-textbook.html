The story

Atoms are far too small to count, so chemists count them by weighing, the way a bank weighs a bag of coins (4.1). The same counting tells you how much of a compound is each element (4.2), and turns the masses from an experiment into a formula (4.3).

The mole is a count

A mole is a number, like a dozen, only far bigger.

A dozen eggs is 12 eggs, whatever size the eggs are. A mole works the same way: one mole of anything is 6.022 × 1023 of it. That number is chosen so the mass of one mole, in grams, matches the formula mass you already know.

Picture it · particles

A dozen water molecules: 12 H2O. A mole is the same idea with 6.022 × 1023 of them.

One mole = 6.022 × 1023 particles, of whatever you name: atoms, molecules or ions.
2.000 mol H2O = 2.000 × 6.022 × 1023× 6.022 × 1023
