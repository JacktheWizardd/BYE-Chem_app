Drafting table, three technical directions

Each concept keeps the Drafting table look (paper, hairline grid, matte clay oxygen and slate nitrogen) and adds a different kind of precision: measurements drawn on the model, live instrument readings, or true atom sizes in a cut-away. All views are real 3D: drag to turn, scroll to zoom.

Numbers are real: C–H bond 1.09 Å, H–C–H angle 109.5°, Bondi van der Waals radii, and gas speeds drawn from the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution for nitrogen and oxygen. The gas box is shown 4.8 × 3.0 × 3.0 nm; molecules there are drawn about three times their true size so you can see them.

  • Teaches: that ball-and-stick is a simplification; real atoms are fuzzy and overlap. Good for Chapters 2, 7 and 10.
  • Feels: the most striking of the three; reads like a museum cut-away model.
  • Risk: see-through shells cost more on older phones; turn them off in big scenes.
In the engine: one clipping plane and transparent matte shells; no custom shaders, so it runs anywhere three.js does.

They combine well. A natural split: Engineering drawing as the default look for molecules and Learn pictures, Instrument readout inside the gas and reaction sandboxes, and Cut-away as a toggle (“show real size”) on any molecule. All three share the same paper, grid, colours and materials, so they read as one app.
