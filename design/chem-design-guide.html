Everything a working chemist reads, writes and measures, drawn the BYE Chem way: the 図 Drafting table look with its three technical layers (engineering drawing, instrument readout, cut-away). Notation follows IUPAC conventions so students learn the forms they’ll meet in labs and papers. 1 · The look paper, hairlines, matte atoms, letters on every atom

Element colours muted CPK families

Chemists expect oxygen red, nitrogen blue, sulfur yellow, chlorine green (the Jmol/CPK families). Same families, redrawn for paper; every atom has an ink outline and a letter with at least 4.5:1 contrast.

2 · Notation the forms chemists actually write

Equations IUPAC

2H2(g) + O2(g) → 2H2O(l)
N2(g) + 3H2(g) Fe, 450 °C⇌ 2NH3(g)
Fe3+(aq) + 3OH−(aq) → Fe(OH)3(s)
  • → reaction, ⇌ equilibrium, ↔ resonance only.
  • Conditions above or below the arrow; state symbols in brackets, never subscript.
  • Charge: number then sign, raised: SO42−.

  • Italic quantity symbols (p, V, n, T, c), upright units and element symbols.
  • A space between number and unit; none before K.
  • ×10n, never “E”; sig figs follow the data.

  • Oxidation states: Roman numerals in names; signed numbers above atoms while assigning.

Graph axes quantity / unit

  • Label as T / K, V(NaOH) / mL, so tick values are plain numbers.
  • The plain name can lead: “Temperature, T / °C”.

4 · Structures in 2D IUPAC 2008 drawing standards

Lewis structure every valence electron

  • Bonds as lines, lone pairs as dot pairs, formal charge circled beside the atom.

Skeletal formula organic chemistry

  • Zig-zag at 120°; heteroatoms labelled with their H.
  • Solid wedge toward the viewer, hashed wedge away; narrow end at the stereocentre.

Resonance ↔ and brackets

[ O=N–O−  ↔  −O–N=O ]
  • Nitrite ion, NO2− (lone pairs left out here): two drawings of one real structure. The double-headed ↔ means resonance, never equilibrium.

8 · Lab: reading instruments and staying safe

Reading a buret bottom of the meniscus

  • Read the bottom of the meniscus at eye level; estimate one digit past the marks (0.01 mL on a 0.1 mL buret).
  • The same rule sets significant figures for every instrument: balance, pipet, graduated cylinder.

Safety information words first

DANGER
Hydrochloric acid, 6 M
Causes severe skin burns and eye damage. Wear goggles and gloves; add acid to water, never water to acid.
WARNING
Ethanol
Highly flammable liquid and vapour. Keep away from flames.
  • Signal word, hazard and what to do, in plain words. The official GHS pictograms are referenced from the real label, not redrawn.
  • The app is for learning, never a substitute for the lab’s safety sheet.
