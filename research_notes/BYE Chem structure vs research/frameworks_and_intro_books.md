# Official chemistry frameworks and introductory textbook orders (vs BYE Chem order)

## AP Chemistry CED (effective Fall 2024): units, topics, and where key items sit

### Takeaway
AP Chemistry (CED V.1, (c) 2024 College Board) has 9 units. It puts atomic structure (incl. moles, mass spec, electron configuration, PES) first, then compound structure/bonding (Lewis, VSEPR/hybridization), then IMFs/gases/solutions/spectroscopy, then reactions, kinetics, thermochemistry, equilibrium, acids/bases, thermodynamics/electrochemistry. The College Board says the order is one optional sequence, not a requirement.

### Cited Findings
Source for everything in this section: [AP Chemistry CED PDF (College Board)](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap-chemistry-course-and-exam-description.pdf), read from the downloaded PDF text (Course Framework pp. 15-19 and unit guides). Weightings are of the multiple-choice section.

- Units, exam weighting, suggested class periods (45 min x 5 days/week):
  1. Atomic Structure and Properties, 7-9%, ~9-10 periods
  2. Compound Structure and Properties, 7-9%, ~12-13
  3. Properties of Substances and Mixtures, 18-22%, ~14-15
  4. Chemical Reactions, 7-9%, ~14-15
  5. Kinetics, 7-9%, ~13-14
  6. Thermochemistry, 7-9%, ~10-11
  7. Equilibrium, 7-9%, ~13-15
  8. Acids and Bases, 11-15%, ~14-16
  9. Thermodynamics and Electrochemistry, 7-9%, ~10-13 (periods figure for unit 9 partly garbled in extraction; read as ~10-13)
- Sequence status: units "have been arranged in a logical sequence frequently found in many college courses and textbooks"; the suggested sequence came from syllabi of highly effective AP teachers and the organization of typical college textbooks; "experienced AP teachers ... should feel no pressure to adopt these units, which comprise an optional sequence."
- Unit 1 topics: 1.1 Moles and Molar Mass; 1.2 Mass Spectra of Elements; 1.3 Elemental Composition of Pure Substances; 1.4 Composition of Mixtures; 1.5 Atomic Structure and Electron Configuration; 1.6 Photoelectron Spectroscopy; 1.7 Periodic Trends; 1.8 Valence Electrons and Ionic Compounds.
- Unit 2 topics: 2.1 Types of Chemical Bonds; 2.2 Intramolecular Force and Potential Energy; 2.3 Structure of Ionic Solids; 2.4 Structure of Metals and Alloys; 2.5 Lewis Diagrams; 2.6 Resonance and Formal Charge; 2.7 VSEPR and Hybridization.
- Unit 3 topics: 3.1 Intermolecular and Interparticle Forces; 3.2 Properties of Solids; 3.3 Solids, Liquids, and Gases; 3.4 Ideal Gas Law; 3.5 Kinetic Molecular Theory; 3.6 Deviation from Ideal Gas Law; 3.7 Solutions and Mixtures; 3.8 Representations of Solutions; 3.9 Separation of Solutions and Mixtures (Chromatography); 3.10 Solubility; 3.11 Spectroscopy and the Electromagnetic Spectrum; 3.12 Properties of Photons; 3.13 Beer-Lambert Law. - Unit 4 topics: 4.1 Introduction for Reactions; 4.2 Net Ionic Equations; 4.3 Representations of Reactions; 4.4 Physical and Chemical Changes; 4.5 Stoichiometry; 4.6 Introduction to Titration; 4.7 Types of Chemical Reactions; 4.8 Introduction to Acid-Base Reactions; 4.9 Oxidation-Reduction (Redox) Reactions.
- Unit 5 topics: 5.1 Reaction Rates; 5.2 Introduction to Rate Law; 5.3 Concentration Changes Over Time; 5.4 Elementary Reactions; 5.5 Collision Model; 5.6 Reaction Energy Profile; 5.7 Introduction to Reaction Mechanisms; 5.8 Reaction Mechanism and Rate Law; 5.9 Pre-Equilibrium Approximation; 5.10 Multistep Reaction Energy Profile; 5.11 Catalysis.

- Unit 8 topics: 8.1 Introduction to Acids and Bases; 8.2 pH and pOH of Strong Acids and Bases; 8.3 Weak Acid and Base Equilibria; 8.4 Acid-Base Reactions and Buffers; 8.5 Acid-Base Titrations; 8.6 Molecular Structure of Acids and Bases; 8.7 pH and pKa; 8.8 Properties of Buffers; 8.9 Henderson-Hasselbalch Equation; 8.10 Buffer Capacity; 8.11 pH and Solubility.
- Unit 9 topics: 9.1 Introduction to Entropy; 9.2 Absolute Entropy and Entropy Change; 9.3 Gibbs Free Energy and Thermodynamic Favorability; 9.4 Thermodynamic and Kinetic Control; 9.5 Free Energy and Equilibrium; 9.6 Free Energy of Dissolution; 9.7 Coupled Reactions; 9.8 Galvanic (Voltaic) and Electrolytic Cells; 9.9 Cell Potential and Free Energy; 9.10 Cell Potential Under Nonstandard Conditions; 9.11 Electrolysis and Faraday's Law.
- Where named items sit: moles 1.1 (first topic of the course); mass spec 1.2; PES 1.6 (after electron configuration 1.5); Lewis diagrams 2.5; VSEPR+hybridization 2.7; IMFs 3.1; ideal gases/KMT 3.4-3.6; Beer-Lambert 3.13 (after photons 3.12); first titration 4.6 and acid-base titration 8.5. Stoichiometry (4.5) comes after moles (1.1) by three units.
- Unit 1 intro frames it as the foundation: macroscopic samples need "moles to translate between this and the particulate scale"; periodic-table organization and electron configuration are described there.
- Atomic before compound structure in AP: yes. Unit 1 (atom, electron config, PES, periodic trends) precedes Unit 2 (bonding, Lewis, VSEPR, hybridization). Lewis/VSEPR is placed after electron configuration; there is no Lewis-before-quantum order.

### Inferences
- AP is effectively an "atoms first" order with moles pulled forward to the very start; BYE Chem is a classic "reactions/mole first, then bonding, then quantum" order. For Chem 1A/AP-aligned later chapters this is a real sequencing difference (AP puts quantum/PES ahead of Lewis/VSEPR).
- Topics in AP not usually early in a classic gen-chem order: mass spectrometry (1.2), PES (1.6), coupled reactions (9.7), pre-equilibrium approximation (5.9), thermodynamic vs kinetic control (9.4), Beer-Lambert and photon properties (3.12-3.13), chromatography/separation (3.9), molecular structure of acids/bases (8.6), pH and solubility (8.11), structure of metals/alloys (2.4).
- AP places gases, IMFs and solutions in one unit (Unit 3), and thermochemistry (6) after kinetics (5) and before equilibrium (7); BYE has thermochem at Ch 8 (early), kinetics at 17.

## ACS Exams Institute Anchoring Concepts Content Map (ACCM), general chemistry

### Takeaway
ACCM organizes general chemistry content under 10 "anchoring concepts" (big ideas) and is a content map for exam-item alignment, not a teaching sequence. I could not retrieve the list of the ten names from an accessible primary source (ACS pages returned 403).

## Introductory/preparatory textbooks: chapter orders

### Takeaway
All mainstream intro books put measurement/problem solving early (ch 2) with math inside that chapter or as an appendix, not a stand-alone chapter 0. Mole/composition and reactions precede bonding. In every one except Ball, quantum/electrons-in-atoms (a descriptive, simplified level) come before bonding; bonding chapters then use Lewis/VSEPR-style content. Gases come before bonding in Corwin and Ball, after it in Tro and Zumdahl.

### Inferences
- Math placement: no book found has a "Chapter 0" math review. Tro's and Zumdahl's measurement chapter (ch 2) carries calculation skills (and Tro Global lists an appendix); Corwin's ch 15 "Advanced Problem Solving" is a mid-book problem-solving chapter. BYE's Ch 0 math chapter is therefore unusual among intro books; closest analogue is Tro/Zumdahl ch 2 plus appendix.
- Mole placement: in Tro and Corwin the mole follows reactions/composition; Zumdahl puts reactions and aqueous reactions (6-7) before composition and quantities (8-9); Ball puts the mole after reactions in one stoichiometry chapter. BYE puts the mole (Ch 4) before reactions (Ch 5), unlike all four books, which put reactions first or together with the mole.
- Quantum/electrons: Tro (ch 9 Electrons in Atoms) and Corwin (ch 4 Models of the Atom, ch 5 Periodic Table) place electron structure before bonding; Zumdahl places Modern Atomic Theory (11) immediately before Chemical Bonding (12); Ball places Electronic Structure (ch 9 in the list) before Chemical Bonds (ch 10). So all four teach atoms/electrons before Lewis/VSEPR, which is the opposite of BYE Ch 9 (bonds and shapes) before Ch 10 (light and the electron). Caveat: I only read chapter titles; I did not verify whether the "electrons" chapters cover orbitals or only energy levels/electron-dot level, nor whether the bonding chapters rely on them (UNVERIFIED).
- Gases: before liquids/solids/solutions in all four books; after bonding in Tro and Zumdahl; before bonding in Corwin (ch 10 vs 12) and Ball (Gases ch 7, bonds ch 10). BYE puts gases (Ch 7) before bonding, matching Corwin and Ball.
- Solutions then acids/bases then equilibrium is the common intro order (Tro 13-15; Zumdahl 15-17; Corwin 13,14,16; Ball 12-14). Intro books defer equilibrium until after acids and bases (Tro, Corwin, Ball: acids/bases before equilibrium), whereas BYE puts equilibrium (18) before acids/bases (19), the AP and Chem 1A order.
- Nuclear, organic, biochemistry are final chapters in all four; BYE moves organic earlier (16) and nuclear to 23.
- Thermochemistry/energy: Tro puts energy in ch 3 (with matter); Corwin ch 3; Zumdahl ch 10; Ball ch 8. BYE Ch 8 is mid-book, similar to Zumdahl/Ball.
