# Sequencing of general chemistry topics: research evidence vs. the BYE Chem order

Method note: web search returned summaries; several publisher pages (RSC, ACS) returned 403. Findings below come from abstracts/ERIC records/summaries, NOT full texts, unless stated. Effect sizes are mostly not available to me; where absent this is flagged. Items marked [UNVERIFIED] came from search summaries or secondary citations and should be checked before quoting.

## 1. Atoms-first vs traditional sequencing: comparative outcomes

### Takeaway
Evidence is mixed and low-to-moderate in quality (all quasi-experimental, single-institution, confounded by instructor/exam/textbook change). Two large studies found atoms-first did no better or worse overall (Esterling & Bartels: initial pass-rate drop; Hillesheim: traditional higher); a 600-student study found favorable results on structure topics only. Nothing found shows atoms-first helps underprepared students. Note: the "atoms-first" in these studies means electron/atomic structure (quantum-ish) FIRST, which is closer to the opposite of BYE Chem ch 9-10 (Lewis/VSEPR before quantum).

### Cited Findings
- Esterling & Bartels (2013), >8,000 students over five years, about two-fifths taught atoms-first (UC Riverside-type quarter system): early on a smaller fraction of atoms-first students passed Q1 and Q2; after a year the Q1 gap was more than offset, but Q2 difficulties persisted; authors present it as "a cautionary example." Esterling, K. M.; Bartels, L. J. Chem. Educ. 2013, 90(11), 1433-1436. [ERIC record](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1028492); DOI https://doi.org/10.1021/ed300725m. (Effect sizes not obtained.)
- Chitiyo, Potter & Rezsnyak (2018), ~600 students over three years, switch from traditional reaction-based to atoms-first (electronic structure first, used to explain reactions and stoichiometry later in semester 1), measured by ACS exams: outcomes "generally favorable for topics in atomic and molecular structure"; abstract does not claim overall gains. J. Chem. Educ. 2018, 95(10), 1711-1716. [ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1195639); DOI https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jchemed.8b00195. (Per-topic effect sizes not obtained.)
- Hillesheim (2016) PhD dissertation, Mississippi State (not peer reviewed): >9,500 Gen Chem I/II students, 7 semesters, ~half atoms-first; ACS final exam and course grades both higher for the TRADITIONAL approach in both courses, across gender, above/below-average GPA, and large classes (ANCOVA/regression); author notes confounds (final-exam content, new-teacher effects, withdrawal timing) and that atoms-first textbooks better matched research-based practice. [Scholars Junction](https://scholarsjunction.msstate.edu/td/1120); handle https://hdl.handle.net/11668/20251.
- Molina (2014) dissertation, University of North Texas (Collin College) (not peer reviewed): atoms-first students did better, statistically significant, on ACS-item topics of descriptive chemistry/periodicity, molecular structure and stoichiometry; single institution. [UNT](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc799539/m2/1/high_res_d/dissertation.pdf) (details via search summary only [UNVERIFIED]).
- Position piece: Scerri, "Should atoms be put first in teaching general chemistry and if so how?" in Orian (ed.), Perspectives in Chemical Education (Padua Univ. Press). Argues for an atoms-first course framed historically (laws of chemical combination and the periodic table first); notes the philosophical objection that atoms-first courses tend to be ahistorical. Opinion. [Scerri page](https://ericscerri.com/articles.html); copy via [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/366275605_Should_atoms_be_put_first_in_teaching_general_chemistry_and_if_so_how) (not read).
- Large-scale DFW perspective: lowest DFW (~5%) occurs where active learning, core-concepts curriculum, and low-stakes assessment with instructor intervention are combined, i.e. pedagogy/assessment matter at least as much as topic order. "What Moves the Needle on DFW Rates and Student Success in General Chemistry? A Quarter-Century Perspective," J. Chem. Educ. (summary only, [catalog record](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_journals_2800248546)) [UNVERIFIED details].

### Inferences
- Topic order alone has weak and inconsistent effects on overall performance; implementation, instructor and assessment confound everything.
- Structure/bonding topics benefit when taught early with deliberate scaffolding; this loosely supports BYE Chem putting bonds/shapes early.
- No evidence supports switching BYE Chem to quantum-first; the cautionary result (Esterling & Bartels) is about abstract content first for weaker-prepared students.

### Gaps
- Could not obtain full texts, effect sizes (Cohen's d), or attrition numbers for Esterling & Bartels or Chitiyo et al.
- No 2019-2025 comparative atoms-first study located (search was limited). No study found specific to underprepared students or to a dedicated "Lewis before quantum" sequence.

## 2. CLUE (structure, properties, energy) and bonding before/after quantum

### Takeaway
CLUE is organized around three interwoven learning progressions (structure, properties, energy) with energy running throughout, and has quasi-experimental evidence of better Lewis-structure construction/decoding and structure-property reasoning. CLUE treats Lewis structures as models carrying information early, supporting early teaching of Lewis structures coupled to properties. It does not (per what I verified) frame the issue as Lewis-before-quantum.

### Cited Findings
- Cooper & Klymkowsky (2013) describe CLUE as a two-semester course and a model for curriculum reform. J. Chem. Educ. 2013, 90(9), 1116-1122, DOI 10.1021/ed300456y. [CU Experts record](https://experts.colorado.edu/display/pubid_54679) (DOI from secondary listing [UNVERIFIED]; paywalled).
- Cooper & Klymkowsky (2013) CBE-LSE paper names three interconnected learning progressions, structure, properties and energy; the energy progression "begins in chapter 1 and extends through the whole curriculum"; bonding is framed as a continuum with potential-energy minimization; longitudinal outcome studies described as ongoing; no outcome data in that paper. Cooper, M. M.; Klymkowsky, M. W. CBE Life Sci. Educ. 2013, 12(2), 306-312. [PMC3671656](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3671656); DOI https://doi.org/10.1187/cbe.12-10-0170.
- Lewis structure skills: CLUE students showed "marked improvements (effect size r = 0.6)" over control students in constructing structures and were better at decoding information in them (as summarized by search tool; original is Cooper, Grove, Underwood & Klymkowsky 2010 and later work) [UNVERIFIED; primary citation not confirmed].
- Underwood, Reyes-Gastelum & Cooper (2016), IILSI instrument, longitudinal: it takes multiple semesters for students in a traditional curriculum to recognize that structures predict properties; CLUE students make the connection earlier; traditional students "fall behind early and never catch up." Chem. Educ. Res. Pract. 2016, 17(2). [RSC](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2016/rp/c5rp00217f) (DOI 10.1039/C5RP00217F; page returned 403, details from search summary).
- Williams, Underwood, Klymkowsky & Cooper (2015): CLUE students were significantly more likely to say IMFs act between rather than within small molecules (J. Chem. Educ. 2015; details via search summary [UNVERIFIED]).
- High-school version (HS-CLUE) associated with viewing Lewis structures as models, hydrogen bonds as "between" molecules, and better boiling-point explanations; associational. [Bohrium listing](https://bohrium.dp.tech/paper/arxiv/812729534548803585).
- MSU reports a ~20% higher pass rate after replacing traditional general chemistry with CLUE (~700 students/year); institutional claim, not controlled. [MSU Today](https://msutoday.msu.edu/news/2015/msu-chemistry-courses-improve-learning-save-students-money).

### Inferences
- Gains in CLUE come with full curriculum + pedagogy change (active learning, assessment), so order effects cannot be isolated.
- CLUE's early structure-to-property emphasis supports BYE Chem moving from shapes/polarity (ch 9) to properties, but BYE Chem puts IMFs at ch 13, much later than CLUE would; consider a short structure-to-property link (polarity -> boiling point) in ch 9.
- CLUE puts energy throughout from chapter 1, whereas BYE Chem's energy appears at ch 8 and bond energetics at ch 12.

### Gaps
- Primary effect-size sources for CLUE (r = 0.6) not verified. No CLUE paper found explicitly testing Lewis-before-orbitals.

## 3. Learning progressions, spiral curricula, "first look then revisit"

### Takeaway
Learning-progression and chemical-thinking frameworks support revisiting concepts at increasing sophistication, but I found no controlled study isolating "first look at X, full treatment later" in general chemistry.

### Cited Findings
- Sevian & Talanquer (2014) propose a learning progression on "chemical thinking" organized by crosscutting practices (analyzing, synthesizing, transforming matter) from grade 8 to undergraduate, arguing dominant curricula teach isolated concepts. Chem. Educ. Res. Pract. 2014, 15, 10-23. DOI https://doi.org/10.1039/C3RP00111C; [RSC](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2014/rp/c3rp00111c). Perspective/theory, not an outcomes study.
- Bernholt & Sevian (2018) commentary compares learning progressions and teaching-learning sequences; cautions on equating them. CERP 2018. [RSC](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2018/rp/c8rp90009d) (not read in detail).
- Talanquer's "Chemical Thinking" general chemistry (University of Arizona) is implemented; sequence not obtained.

### Inferences
- A "first look at pH" in ch 6 then full treatment in ch 19 fits spiral/progression theory, but the evidence is conceptual. Risk: initial simplified models (e.g., acids as just H+ donors) may need explicit revisiting.

### Gaps
- No experimental evidence for spiral vs blocked in chemistry located; Johnstone's levels (macro/submicro/symbolic) not searched in primary form this round.

## 4. The mole: placement and difficulty

### Takeaway
The mole is widely documented as a major difficulty and a bottleneck for stoichiometry; I found no study comparing a dedicated mole chapter vs distributed treatment, or mole-before-stoichiometry ordering experiments.

### Cited Findings
- Furió, Azcona, Guisasola & Ratcliffe (2000): examine difficulties teaching "amount of substance" and "mole"; link student difficulty to teachers' ignorance of the concepts' historical construction; mole poorly understood hampers stoichiometry. Int. J. Sci. Educ. 2000, 22(12), 1285-1304. [paper copy](https://staff.fnwi.uva.nl/e.joling/vakdidactiek/documenten/furio.pdf); [record](https://ekoizpen-zientifikoa.ehu.eus/documentos/5eb2ca4329995223401a795f).
- Furió, Azcona & Guisasola (2002) literature review reports students have "great difficulty" with these concepts. Chem. Educ. Res. Pract. 2002, 3(3). [RSC](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2002/rp/b2rp90023h).
- Ralph & Lewis (2018): students in the bottom quartile of math aptitude showed the largest performance gaps on mole-concept and stoichiometry items. Chem. Educ. Res. Pract. 2018, 19, 867-884, "Chemistry topics posing incommensurate difficulty to students with low math aptitude scores." DOI 10.1039/C8RP00115D; [RSC](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/Content/ArticleLanding/RP/2018/C8RP00115D); [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10095452). Sample size and effect sizes not obtained.
- Ralph & Lewis (2019) follow-up analyzing handwritten solutions to explain the gap. CERP 2019. DOI 10.1039/C9RP00068B; [RSC author version](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/getauthorversionpdf/c9rp00068b).
- Dori & Hameiri (1998/2003) on mole and stoichiometry (cited in Furió; not independently searched) [UNVERIFIED].

### Inferences
- The mole + stoichiometry cluster is the most math-sensitive place in the course, supporting extra time and embedded math support there. A separate chapter is consistent with the evidence but not tested against alternatives.

### Gaps
- No experimental evidence on dedicated vs distributed mole teaching. Dori & Hameiri not read.

## 5. Math preparation: predictive power and up-front vs just-in-time

### Takeaway
Math skills reliably correlate with general chemistry grades (strong, replicated, correlational evidence). Short math reviews improve math-skill test scores; effects on course grades are unclear. Just-in-time vs up-front has not been directly compared in what I found.

### Cited Findings
- Williamson et al. (2020): MUST (Math-Up Skills Test; 15-minute, calculator-free) with 1,073 students at six Texas institutions; math skills correlate with course grades; poor arithmetic automaticity may underlie lower performance. "Impact of basic arithmetic skills on success in first-semester general chemistry," Chem. Educ. Res. Pract. 2020, 21(1). [RSC](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2020/rp/c9rp00077a) (DOI 10.1039/C9RP00077A; page 403, details from search summary). Note: the "Williamson 2020" cited in the app is in CERP, not JCE.
- Powell, Williamson et al. (2020): 1,599 second-semester students; MUST flags at-risk students. CERP 2020. [RSC](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2020/rp/d0rp00006j).
- Alivio, Howard, Mamiya & Williamson (2020): math review in week 2 of fall semester gave significantly greater MUST gains than a chemistry review; final course averages did not differ significantly (both groups later got both reviews). J. Sci. Educ. Technol. 2020. Title "How Does a Math Review Impact a Student's Arithmetic Skills and Performance in First-Semester General Chemistry?" [catalog record](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsair,edsair.doi...........7154474eb2eca74dde0bdcf31e409899).
- Lewis & Lewis (2007): >3,000 students; formal reasoning (TOLT) and SAT both identify at-risk students, each capturing a separate barrier; later papers cite SAT-math bottom quartile predictions as ~65-75% correct [UNVERIFIED]. "Predicting at-risk students in general chemistry: comparing formal thought to a general achievement measure," Chem. Educ. Res. Pract. 2007, 8, 32-51. DOI https://doi.org/10.1039/B6RP90018F; [RSC](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2007/rp/b6rp90018f). Note: CERP, not JCE.
- Just-in-time: Gayle (2024) dissertation, 40 students, in-class math review raised MUST scores and self-efficacy (small, action research; [record](https://scholarcommons.sc.edu/etd/8214) [UNVERIFIED]). Bowser (2011) Montana State project: direct math instruction improved performance on multi-variable/unit-conversion tasks by up to 28% ([record](https://scholarworks.montana.edu/handle/1/959)); small, not rigorous.

### Inferences
- Prediction by math supports a Ch 0, but the evidence for a standalone up-front math review improving grades is thin; reviews improve math scores. A hybrid (short Ch 0 for arithmetic automaticity/algebra/units + deferred logs/quadratics at point of use) is consistent with the literature, though never head-to-head tested.
- Arithmetic automaticity (not advanced math) is the documented predictor, so Ch 0 should emphasize that over logs/quadratics.

### Gaps
- No controlled comparison of just-in-time vs up-front math review. Effect sizes for Williamson/Ralph & Lewis not retrieved.

## 6. Lewis/VSEPR before orbitals

### Takeaway
No study found directly comparing "Lewis/VSEPR before quantum" with "quantum first"; the literature documents persistent difficulty in the Lewis-to-3D step and rote hybridization, and debates whether to integrate or drop orbital models.

### Cited Findings
- Students tend to describe shape from Lewis structures alone without full geometry (2023 Indonesian study, n=701; [journal PDF](https://journal.uinsgd.ac.id/index.php/tadris-kimiya/article/download/24410/pdf)) [UNVERIFIED details].
- Pinhas, Kugel & Jensen (2024) argue textbooks present VSEPR and hybridization as separate topics and aim to link them (Int. J. Chem. [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ibn/ijcjnl/v16y2024i1p57.html)).
- Lamoureux & Ogilvie (2022) propose eliminating hybridization and VSEPR from intro/organic courses (position piece, summarized via search tool [UNVERIFIED]).
- Position: Lewis structures should be taught for predictive utility, not as an end ([RACI Chemistry in Australia, 2018](https://chemaust.raci.org.au/article/june-2018/lewis-structures-ban-octet-rule.html)). Opinion.
- CLUE treats Lewis structures as models carrying property information (section 2).

### Inferences
- BYE Chem's order is defensible but should include an explicit bridge back (ch 12) so students don't treat Lewis and orbital models as unrelated, as in the Pinhas et al. critique.

### Gaps
- No empirical test of sequencing here. Honest statement: evidence is absent.

## 7. Placement of thermochemistry, gases, kinetics/equilibrium, organic

### Takeaway
I found no empirical sequencing studies on these topics. Only CLUE (energy throughout from the start) offers a rationale.

### Cited Findings
- CLUE spreads energy through the curriculum from chapter 1 ([PMC3671656](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3671656)).

### Gaps
- Nothing located on gases early vs late, kinetics before equilibrium, or intro organic placement. Further search needed (e.g., ACS Exams Institute topic-order reports, Murphy et al. on topic-order across texts).
